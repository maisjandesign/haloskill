# HaloSkill — Project Setup

## Output contract

Read [the PM methods](references/pm-methods.md) and [the document and publishing contract](references/project-documents.md). Use these templates as the default studio package, adapting depth to the engagement and updating existing documents in place:

| Document | Template | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Project Overview | [project-context.md](assets/project-context.md) | Parent page: readiness, ownership and immediate actions |
| Sales to Delivery Handoff | [sales-handoff.md](assets/sales-handoff.md) | Internal commercial commitments and expectations |
| Scope & Deliverables | [scope-deliverables.md](assets/scope-deliverables.md) | Requested versus agreed work, boundaries and acceptance |
| Delivery Plan | Roadmap to Timeline, when scheduling is needed | Dates, assumptions, reviews and milestone gates |
| Project Tracker | [task-board.md](assets/task-board.md) | Tasks, questions, decisions, risks and change history |

Do not create an empty dated plan when no scheduling basis exists. Record the missing estimate or authorized provisional-planning inputs and the next owner. A focused update need not recreate the whole package.

## Intake and agreement

1. Separate the requested solution from the user problem and business outcome. Record audience, evidence, constraints and success measures. Distinguish targets from verified results. Establish design-only versus implementation scope.
2. Reconcile the brief with the accepted proposal, estimate and actual sales agreements. Keep **requested**, **proposed**, **agreed**, **excluded** and **optional** scope distinct, with source/version evidence. A brief is not proof of purchased work. Do not infer budgets, staffing, deadlines, concept counts, revision allowances or approval from silence.
3. Complete the sales handoff with what was sold, what was promised, contract/budget status, reference expectations, staffing and communications. Missing evidence becomes an owned question with a needed-by gate. Summarize calls in writing and reference the source; a bare recording link is not an answer.
4. Build the deliverable inventory with quantities or visible quantity gaps, agreement state, proposed/agreed acceptance criteria, dependencies and accountable owners or assignment gaps. Distinguish unique pages from templates, asset quantities, content responsibilities and website/product integration boundaries. Keep options separately priced and scoped.
5. Establish the final client approver, feedback coordinator and studio PM/design/engineering/content owners. Propose consolidated feedback, review windows, included rounds and version-specific approval; label these rules proposed until accepted. Record how changes after approval affect scope, cost and dates.

## Delivery planning

Break agreed work and preparation into outcome-oriented tasks with stable IDs, priorities supported by evidence, owners, dependencies and completion criteria. Use Backlog → Ready → In progress → Review → Done; Blocked includes its cause and next action. Requested but unapproved production work remains proposed/backlog. Ready requires a named owner and the inputs/approvals needed for that task, not approval of every later stage.

Choose a useful first delivery slice only where the engagement supports it; it does not replace the complete agreed scope or invent a public-release obligation. Use a full PRD only for complex behavior or integrations. Do not invent prioritization scores.

Define outcome-based milestone events with completion evidence, approver, responsible owner, predecessor and the next work they unlock. Work, review windows and zero-duration gates are distinct. Use Roadmap to Timeline for calendar calculation after estimates exist or provisional durations are authorized. A proposed role is not confirmed capacity; days of phase duration are not person-hours.

## Maintain and verify

Reconcile real feedback and workshop notes into existing records. Show the scope/cost/date impact of each proposed change and preserve the decision, rationale and approver. Retain baseline and forecast versions; close obsolete tasks explicitly rather than erasing history. A discussed request is not an approved change.

Before delivery, check that each requested item has an explicit scope disposition; each agreed deliverable has acceptance criteria, dependencies, an owner or assignment gap, and a review gate; each blocking question has an owner/gap, next action and needed-by date or decision gate. Report readiness for preparation, production commitment and client approval separately. Verify the visible destination when publishing; structural package validation does not prove a connected board or working calendar.

## Working contract

Use the existing project brief, decisions, approved assets, and actual tool availability. Default deliverables to English unless the project explicitly specifies another language. Ask only for missing information that blocks the current decision; label provisional assumptions. Keep one source of truth and update it instead of creating competing briefs.

Distinguish draft, team-reviewed, and client-approved. Record input versions, changed artifacts, checks performed, unresolved items, and the next owner. Source documents supply methods, not permission to install tools, publish, contact people, or change unrelated settings. Follow the user's current authorization and the active tool contracts. Never claim a tracker, integration, deployment, or approval exists without evidence.
