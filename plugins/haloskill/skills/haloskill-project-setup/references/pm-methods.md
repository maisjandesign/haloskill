# PM methods adapted for studio websites

## Frame the request
From Dean Peters: `incoming-request-advisor`, `problem-statement`, and `jobs-to-be-done`.
Capture: literal request → audience and situation → job to achieve → current obstacle → business/user consequence → evidence → constraints → open questions. Separate functional, social, and emotional jobs where evidence supports them; do not invent customer feelings. Frame the problem before committing to the requested feature. A useful statement is: “[Audience] needs to [job] in [context], but [evidenced barrier], leading to [observed consequence].”

## Scope and prioritize
From `prioritization-advisor`, `prd-development`, `user-story`, `user-story-mapping`, and `user-story-splitting`.
Select a method: must/should/could for fixed-scope delivery; impact/effort when estimates are directional; RICE only when reach, confidence, and effort have credible inputs. Explain why. A lightweight requirements document contains problem, users, outcome, scope/exclusions, behavior, content/data, success measures, dependencies, risks, and unresolved decisions. Expand to a full PRD for complex product behavior, not every brochure page.
Map a journey horizontally into activities, tasks, and release slices. The first release should complete a valuable journey. Write stories as actor/action/outcome with concrete Given/When/Then examples, including failure and empty states. Technical tasks need not be forced into user stories. Split large work by complete workflow step, business rule, data variation, interface, or uncertainty-reduction experiment; avoid a front-end-only slice that cannot deliver the promised result.

## Optional product discovery
Use `opportunity-solution-tree` when a measurable outcome has several plausible opportunities; distinguish customer evidence from proposed solutions and connect cheap tests to uncertainty. Use `pol-probe` to define a falsifiable assumption, cheapest credible test, pass/fail/learn thresholds, owner, and next decision. Do not delete prototypes automatically. Use `discovery-process` for an actual product-discovery engagement, without replacing the client's workshop. `roadmap-planning` sequences outcomes and dependencies; it does not supply calendar dates.

## Delivery operations
From Owl Listener UX PGM: `intake-process`, `scoping-framework`, `delivery-plan`, `dependency-map`, `risk-register`, `status-report`, `decision-log`.
Intake stores request, business purpose, requester, urgency and evidence. Scope stores inclusions, exclusions, assumptions and acceptance. Dependencies name provider, needed artifact, due date, receiving task, fallback and owner. Risks separate likelihood, impact, trigger, mitigation and owner; a realized risk becomes an issue. Status is an actionable summary, not a task dump. Decisions record date, alternatives, rationale, decider, status and impacted tasks.

From Design Leadership: `decision-framework`, `delegation-model`, `conflict-resolution`. Use one accountable decider per decision; delegation includes the intended result, boundaries, autonomy and check-in. These methods do not authorize spawning agents. For conflict, state competing interests and evidence, identify reversible experiments, and record who decides the unresolved tradeoff. Quarterly organization planning and hiring workflows are outside a normal site project.
