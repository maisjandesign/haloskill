# HaloSkill — Workshop Client Research

Prepare evidence that makes workshop questions specific and useful. Research deeply; keep the handoff short. This skill supplies the research, not the full call script.

Read [the research handoff example](references/handoff-pattern.md) when preparing research for a workshop writer. If the user provides a skill folder, use that version and its local references. Keep client context separate from the reusable skill.

## Method

1. Read the brief and supplied materials. Extract business goals, audiences, offers, launch status, constraints, content ownership, and decisions already made. Check dates; a plan is not proof of delivery. Do not re-open established brand strategy by default.
2. Inspect the current website and relevant public company materials. Look for differences between the offer, messaging, available proof, audience paths, and calls to action. Attribute product and privacy claims to their publisher; do not present marketing as independent verification.
3. Investigate alternatives for each important audience. Distinguish confirmed competitors, candidate alternatives, search competitors, and communication references. Prefer relevant, recognisable brands in the niche; use famous outside-category brands only when the lesson transfers. No invented businesses or claims about a brand's hidden strategy or success.
4. Explore discovery questions and search intent where relevant. Treat SEO/GEO topics as hypotheses without analytics or search evidence. Never invent demand, rankings, AI citations, conversion rates, or customer interviews.
5. Separate answered sales questions from genuinely open decisions. Distinguish evidence that belongs in a spoken setup from audit or production notes. A draft website is not a reliable source of client business facts; prefer approved materials and mark uncertain details.
6. Turn findings into neutral hypotheses the client can correct. Prioritise findings that change a workshop decision. Stop when each stage has useful evidence or a clearly recorded gap; avoid collecting facts merely to sound informed.

## Handoff

Use the user's language and destination; keep client-facing work in their requested language. Default to a compact research brief containing:
- Business context and settled decisions.
- A small set of findings: **source and date → observation → hypothesis → question angle → intended stage**.
- Up to two or three useful brand references with verified observable examples, relevance, and limits; fewer is fine. Mark whether each is suitable for the spoken script or research only.
- Unknowns and requested evidence, including who could supply it.
- Clickable sources, with publication and access dates distinguished.

Map findings to: website goal; visitor situation; reasons to choose; questions and objections; voice; discovery and page journeys. Assign each major fact one main use to prevent repetitive introductions. Keep facts, client assertions, interpretations, and questions distinguishable. Do not invent missing client information.

The handoff is not a question bank. Supply a few strong facts and question angles, not lists of follow-ups. Record each finding’s intended stage and avoid reusing it in every setup. Keep uncertain numbers, operational checks and migration tasks in preparation notes unless they affect a workshop decision.
