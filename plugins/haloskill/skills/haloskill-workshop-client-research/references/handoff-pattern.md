# Research handoff pattern

This is an illustrative schema, not evidence about a real client. Fill it only from the current project. Keep sources in the research; the writer converts the insight into natural speech without citations.

## Established answers

Record what sales or approved materials already settle: business purpose, main audiences, desired action, known positioning, and content ownership. The workshop should deepen these answers, not restart discovery.

## A useful finding

- Source and date: the supplied sales-call transcript, with its real date or “date not shown”.
- Observation: the client says meetings are the next step after visiting the site.
- Still open: what a buyer needs to understand before agreeing to that meeting.
- Hypothesis: the first page may need to establish relevance and trust before introducing technical detail.
- Spoken angle: place the buyer in the moment after receiving a sales email; ask what would make the meeting worthwhile.
- Main use: Stage 1. Do not repeat this introduction in the later conversion discussion.
- Requested evidence: one recent sales conversation, if the answer stays general.

## Weak handoff

“Discuss goals, customers, proof, objections, tone, SEO. Ask who, why, when, how, and what success means.”

This supplies a checklist but gives the writer no prepared insight.

## Better handoff

Five or six concise findings that identify the source, the unresolved choice, and the stage. Include source links and the distinction between fact and hypothesis. Enough research is not the same as many facts: choose evidence that changes the conversation.

## Brand references

Separate a named sales competitor, a candidate alternative, a search result, and a communication example. A niche competitor can belong in research without becoming an anecdote in the script. Prefer a client-specific hypothesis; use a brand comparison aloud only if the brand is widely recognised or the client has already chosen it and the comparison makes the decision clearer.

## Administrative observations

A missing PDF, migration requirement or undecided CMS may matter to delivery. Keep it in a short project note. Do not automatically turn every issue found during research into another workshop question.
